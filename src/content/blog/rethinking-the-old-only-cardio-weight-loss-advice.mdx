For decades, traditional fitness culture taught us that the only path to shedding weight was hours of steady-state cardio. We were told to get on the elliptical or treadmill, keep our heart rate in a narrow zone, and burn as many calories on the digital display as possible.

While cardiovascular health is critical for heart and lung longevity, relying *exclusively* on cardio for weight management frequently leads to frustration, burnout, and muscle loss.

### Why Cardio-Only Plans Often Stall

When you perform excessive cardio without strength training, your body adapts by becoming more energy-efficient. That means you burn *fewer* calories over time for the same amount of effort. Furthermore, in a caloric deficit, high volumes of cardio can cause your body to break down lean muscle tissue.

Because muscle tissue is metabolically active, losing lean mass lowers your resting metabolic rate (BMR). When your BMR drops, keeping weight off becomes progressively harder.

### The Strength Training Advantage

Resistance training flips this equation. Lifting weights signals your body to preserve and build metabolically active muscle tissue.

Here is why strength training should be the foundation of your routine:

1. **Higher Resting Energy Expenditure:** Muscle burns calories 24/7, even when you are resting, sleeping, or working at your desk.
2. **The EPOC Effect:** After a challenging strength session, your body consumes excess oxygen for hours to repair muscle fibers (Excess Post-Exercise Oxygen Consumption).
3. **Joint & Bone Density:** Resistance exercise strengthens connective tissue and increases bone mineral density, preventing future injuries.
4. **Functional Independence:** Getting stronger makes lifting groceries, climbing stairs in Portland, and enjoying weekend adventures easier and more enjoyable.
